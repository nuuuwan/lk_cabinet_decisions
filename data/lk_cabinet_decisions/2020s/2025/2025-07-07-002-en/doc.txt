Granting approval to the Sri Lanka Navy to conduct Maritime Security Operations

Press briefing of Cabinet Decision taken on 2025-07-07 (Subject to Confirmation at the next Meeting)

- The International Maritime Organization has designated the Somali maritime region, including the Gulf of Aden and the Red Sea as a High‑Risk Area in the year 2010 due to the pirate activities that merchant vessels engaged in international trade have had to face. As a remedy for such threats, merchant vessels entering the High-Risk Area obtain the service of armed sea marshals through foreign private maritime security companies. Although the International Maritime Organization revoked the High-Risk Area status from 2023‑01‑01, commercial vessels have continued to obtain the service of the private maritime security companies. Ports of Galle and Colombo are primarily used for the embarkation and disembarkation of armed sea marshals deployed to protect merchant vessels entering to and departing from the said maritime zones and occasionally the ports of Hambantota and Trincomalee are also used for such maritime security operations. The Sri Lanka Navy has been entrusted with the overall responsibility for national security in carrying out operational activities of the said project. Therefore, taking into consideration the knowledge and experience of the Sri Lanka Navy in relation to maritime security operations, it has been found appropriate to grant approval to the Sri Lanka Navy as well to carry out maritime security operations independently. Accordingly, the proposal made by the Hon. President in his capacity as the Minister of Defence for maritime security operations to be carried out by the Sri Lanka Navy under the supervision of the Ministry of Defence, was approved by the Cabinet.
